Interview Follow Up

I'm sure everyone everwhere is talking about the terrorist attach on NYC 25 years ago. I am aware of it, and remember very destinctly where I was and how I felt when the events happened. I don't feel like I can add anything to that conversation or the historical account of that event. Instead I'll talk about how my interview went!

In short, I don't honestly know. I tried my best to be my authentic self and stay positive, which is something I try to do every day. I also tried to express my heartfelt belief that everything in life can only be clarified with direct communication. I'm not saying be rude to people, but be straight foward in your concerns and needs in clear concise ways that are impossible to be minunderstood.

The position I interviewed for is an IT Project Manager position. It may not be obvious but it's not managing people, but projects and timelines. I know, as someone who has worked in IT for over 10 years, that the hardest part of working in IT and interfacing with others is to communicate clearly and simply in ways that everyone can understand. I also feel like this required voicing questions, which is also a challenge in IT.

There is a stigma in IT where no one wants to admit they don't know something. It's almost like everyone is afraid that if they say they don't know something that they have failed, and I kind of get it. Being an IT worker is a knowledge based job and career. If th ewhole idea of the position is you have knowledge it seems wrong to admit you don't have all the knowledge all the time! The problem though is that if you don't admit you don't know, you don't learn, you don't grow, and in a project management job, it means you are not communicating correctly.

As stated yesterday, I was the most nervous for this job interview of any job interview I have had in over 10 years. I think it was apparent, but about a third of the way in, I made an extra effort to slow down and control myself and the conversation. Hopefully things went well. The person who was running the interview let me know that I should know within a month what their decision on the position and how it relates to me is.

I want to keep an eye out for other jobs, as I don't want to miss out on an opportunity, but I really want this one. I would hate to take a job because they respond and get me on-boarded 2 weeks in, but this one takes a month and I have to burn birdges if I decide I would rather do one over the other.

Working from Home and Pizza

I stated yesterday that I was working from home today. This was so I could take my remote interview from the safety and quiet of my own home, which I'm really glad I did. The whole interview took over an hour, closer to 1.5; but if I had taken it at work I think I would have been interrupted in that time frame. I can scarecly go more than 20 minutes without someone reaching out to me to ask questions.

After the interview though I was able to breath and relax and I finished my work day. Katie went to her sisters house to hang out. At around 7 or so I ordered a small Meat Supreme Pizza and Cheesy Breadsticks from Fox's Pizza Den. It's a chain restaurant, but I think it's most prevalent in Pennsylvania. They have really good pizza though and I don't know if it's stress or what, but it tasted amazing!
