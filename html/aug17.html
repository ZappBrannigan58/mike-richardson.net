Mysteries and Conspiracies

I know I'm not alone, but I really love a good mystery and conspiracy. I don't mean in the sence of like a detective, or Sherlock Holmes type of thing, although there I do enjoy that as well, but I mean it in the sense where everyone involved from experts to skeptics to witnesses are like "WTF!". I'm talking a mystery where everyone is grounded in reality and still no one knows or understands what is going on.'

I was talking to a friend of mine at work recently and he's an avid hunter and outdoorsman. We were talking about his everyday carry, which for those who do not know is a firearm that as the name suggest you carry with you everyday. I'm sure some people, those who are apposed to the ownership of guns in general, will think this sounds threatening and scary, but it's not meant to be, particularly in his situation where he is out in the woods hunting. This particular friend has hunting dongs and mentioned that he keeps the firearm with him when he is out with his dogs to protect them as much as to protect himself. I think, even if you are not an animal lover you can at least respect that level of responsability and compassion.

This converstaion reminded me of a documentary I watched not too long ago called "Missing 411 - Hunters". I didn't realize it at the time but it is based off a series of books written by a former detective. They are all factual and event based, there is no exagerration of the facts, or so I am to understand, and in this particular show/documentary the detective interviews several different people that went missing during hunting expeditions. People go missing, even hunter, I know this; but the way they go missing, and a lot of other factors that surround the events leave those involved just going "WTF?!".

I find it very compelling how the people that go missing, at least in this show, were all experienced hunters. The places they dissapeared from were all places they had been to before and they were familiar with. I personally have never been lost in the woods, and I have done my fair share of tromping through them, but I understand that people go missing, even hunters, particularly when it is an area they are unfamiliar with, but I would think an experienced outdoorsman would know what to do in such an event.

Further in each of these events the person who went missing had a physical disability, either permanent or as a short-term injury. The first gentleman who disappeared was an elderly man who had been hunting his whole life, was military trained, and taught scouts and other hunters about safety and survival tactics. His disability however was that he had only one good eye, gun powder exploded close to his face and caused him to lose the function of one of his eyes. In addition he was an elderly man. In his case he was left in a clearing and a group of hunters were going to try and corral deer toward his location so he could pick them off as they ran by. He had a walkie talkie, was not great walking long distances, and was expereinced. They never found him. Not a foot print, not the walkie talkie, nothing.

There were other examples, but you should just watch the video. You can find it on YouTube Here.
