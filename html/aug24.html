Spider-Man - Brand New Day

Katie and I went this past Saturday with some friends to see the new Spider-Man movie, Brand New Day. I will admit that I am probably a bit bias, as Spider-Man has been my favorite super hero since I was a kid. I had a subscription to the Amazing Spider-Man comic book for several years and also grew up watching the cartoon. Which as far as I can tell followed the comic books pretty closely. I also grew up playing the Maximum Carnage Spider-Man game on the Sega Genesis, which is an Amazing Spider-Man game (see what I did there?). I would argue the best Spider-Man game until the more recent PS4 version.

I also really like the Tom Holland portrayal of the character. I think he makes a pretty convicing Peter Parker, at least way better than the Andrew Garfield version. I think the Tobey Maguire, is probably still the best, and that may be due in part to the time it came out in my life, but overall Tom Holland has a more solid series of films. Spider-Man and Spider-Man 2 were good, but Spider-Man 3 was trash. I also feel like the Andrew Garfield one's fell victim to the Hollywood machine. Meaning that they started to stray too far from the source material and pandered to larger audiences, something that seemed to start with Spider-Man 3. If you not sure what I mean look at the late 80's and 90's BatMan movies. They had 2 really strong movies, but then Hollywood started to get more involved.

What's better than a billion dollar movie franchise? A multi-billion dollar movie franchise and the way to do that is to expand it's appeal by dumbing down the characters, adding non-sensical flashy villians, and just uping the campiness. They Hollywood-ized the films and nearly killed Batman on the big screen. It's basically the same thing that happened in Spongebob Squarepants when Mr.Krabs sold the Krusty Krab and the Crabby Patty turned into a tasteless colorless blob of crap in an effort to "streamline" and generate "mass appeal" Which ultimately just turns the fanbase off the product which generated the money to begin with. It's so obvious I don't understand why executives never seem to get or understand it.

I kind of went on a tangent there, but ultimately I thought it was a good movie. I heard some critical reviews state that the movie was slow to start and doing heavy lifting to start the next phase of the MCU. I don't know about that, I thought the pacing was fine, and a lot of that "slowness" was to build the back story for the villian and make them relatable. The best villians are the ones that blur the lines of what is right and wrong. Look at Thanos. I think we all can get behind reducing the strain on nature, although his overall method was potentially flawed, he was relatable.

My big gripe comes in the blatant advertising throughout the film. It starts with a mini-commercial by Samsung about how Spider-Man needs a better and stronger phone and then pushes their new fold device. I don't care for that, and then throughout the whole film anytime someone was using a phone it was that phone. I know that product placement in films has been a thing for a while, but I don't think that should make it ok in everyones minds. Just because something sucks and has precendent doesn't mean it should be accepted an allowed to continue.

Severe Storms

After our movie ended we went to dinner with our friends and generally had a very good time. If you haven't had the the parmesean encrusted chicken from Longhorn Steak House I would highly recommend it. They have good steak too, but it's too expensive compared to Texas RoadHouse which is right down the street. Our friends like it though because it is much quiter and more intimate, and I can't disagree with that.

While we were sitting there though I heard the thunder before we even left the restaurant. It was loud and bombastic! I knew there was potential for the storm, as I generally watch the news and check the weather app on my phone, but it was insane how much lightning and thunder there was. I don't think I can remember a time where the lightning was so prolific. I really enjoy it personally, but it makes Katie really nervous.

This has been the wettest summer since I've lived in Pittsburgh as well. Which generally I'm ok with, but has made doing all the outdoor activities that I want to do a little challenging. For instance I like to play disc golf and go hiking, both of which get very muddy very fast when it rains like it has been this summer.

I also worry that maybe climates are chaning. Be that from global warming or what have you, and I find that a little concerning as well...

I think it's time we try another sign off...
