What is old even?

I'm a millenial, I'm not sure if that's obvious from my picture of Zapp Brannigan on each Captain's log, or the AI image I used to make myself look cooler on the home page for this site. What I'm finding that means, on a more frequent bassis, is that I'm getting old, or so I'm told! A few of the guys that work for me are under the age of 30, and one in particular and I talk about movies a lot. Another of the guys that works for me is my age and he and I make a lot of references to movies and tv shows that don't seem old to me, or him. When we try to explain what the reference is from we are often told that its old...

My question is what is old even really? I mean at what point does something become old? I know as a child that to me any movie older than like 1989 was ancient. I would immediately be turned away from anything that I deemed old, with a few exceptions. To my young child mind anything older than 15 was old, particularly in movies. I also think that the quality of movies was changing rapidly at the time, as far as visual quality, and while biased I also think in quality fo acting as well. Thats not to say that actors prior to the 80's were not good, I just don't think there was as many and the pool for talent was more restricted. I could get deeper into this, but it's not really the point. During the 90's and early 2000's I think a lot of quality actors and movies were made and are still being talked about today, but also set benchmarks for what a revolutionary movie or actor could be.

Iconic Movies that were realeased in that time are numerous and I mean they are still talked about regularly today:

  • Shawshank Redemption
  • The Matrix
  • Braveheart
  • Fight Club
  • Toy Story
  • Batman
  • Forrest Gump
  • Saving Private Ryan
  • Gladiator
  • The Lord of the Rings
  • Harry Potter

That's not to say that other movies prior too or after were not good and not iconic, but I'm saying The Matrix changed how action movies and sci-fi is made. Toy Story, changed animation as a whole. The Lord of the Rings set the standard for quality fantasy epic movies. These are movies that I don't think could or would ever be re-made because they're too impactful and important to people.

This was all brought up because this younger guy at work had never seen the Matrix. I couldn't believe that. I was discussing the movie "Equalibrium" with the guy closer to my age and we were discussing how if it hadn't come out the same time as the Matrix that we think it would have been more popular and impactful than it was.

Maybe I'm projecting, but maybe things, particularly movies, don't really get old. Maybe they just become classics? It works for cars, why not movies, and maybe even people?

Casual Games

I don't know if the above statement is related to this or not, but I find that lately I've been really enjoying casual games. I'm not talking about games that play themselves on your phone, or that appear to be free, but are designed to be so frustrating that you pay money to make them playable. I'm talking about a game I can play for an 20 minutes and have fun. Also games that do not feel like a job.

Recently I've been playing a game called "Retro Rewind". The premis is that it's 1991 and you just purchased a video rental store. As you may guess you buy movies, build shelves, hire employees, expand the store, stock iventories. It's basically a job simulator, which is something I find a little confusing. When I was younger and working a job like this, not a movie rental store but retail; there is no way I would have wanted to pretend to work in an environment like that for fun. I don't know what it is, but if you take a chore and gamify it, it becomse fun. Look at powerwasher simulator!

Games like this I have to presume are marketed at Millenials like me. We destinctly remember what it was like going to these types of stores, and some of us probably remember what it's like working in stores like this. I have been really enjoying it though and I don't have to make sure I log in every day. Maybe I go a week without playing, maybe I play for 4 hours straight! I don't feel like I have to.

I used to play World of Warcraft, and did so for a very long time. In the War Within expansion I made the decision to step away from it, and I think it will be forever. There were some design changes to the way the game is played that I don't agree with, but more than that I felt pressure to sign in everyday and grind away for hours to actually play the game. Everyone says that WOW starts in endgame, and it does, but up to that point it doesn't respect your time, and to be competitive in endgame you have to spend even more time. I just don't want to commit to a full time job when I should be having fun. It's hard to admit because some of my fondest gaming memories are raiding in WOW back in BC and Wrath, but I guess that's a bygone error and nothing good ever lasts. Which makes me sad.
