Interview Today!

A few weeks ago the IT Director for my company started an meeting with all the managers that report to him by saying we all need to have our resume's updated and have a backup plan. Something I find really distastful and terrible to say to your team. Even if it's true, as we are all working for an independant company that is owned by a private equity. The reality is we could be sold tomorrow, and I think we all know that. But if you are leading a team I don't know what positive impact you expect to have if you make a statement like this.

Since the divestiture happened I have been under a lot of stress. In 2024 Katie was diagnosed with cancer, Seth was learning to drive, and my company was being sold. The cancer obviously is the biggest deal, but since she was unable to work and I need to maintain employment to provide for my family, it was an incredible amount of stress to be under.

Katie is doing much better and recent scans indicate that the immediate threat has been addressed and she should continue to be stuck with me for a very long time!

The reason I bring that up is because I have woken up in the middle of the night by anxiety for the first time in my life, in the past 2 years. Initially it was her cancer, as it was a much more dire threat and is frankly super scary and tough. I wanted to help her as best I could and also maintain what we needed for the family to live comfortably. Now that immediate concern is over, but I still find myself working in an unstable environment that doesn't seem to have a future.

Best case scenario the company I work for gets sold to another company that doesn't have a headquarters/presence in Pittsburgh. They decide they need to keep the entire team and no one gets fired. This is almost impossible and I really don't see it happening. The only way this may happen is if PPG buys us back, which again seems unlikely.

Most likely scenario though, at least I think, is we are sold to company that already has a strong infrastructure in place and the vast majority of employees are found to be redundant and unecessary. Will some keep their jobs? I'm sure a few will, and to be honest I think Site IT is one of the most likely to remain employed, but that doesn't make me feel better.

With all of that said I have been keeping an eye out for potential jobs that I would be interested in. Late last year and earleir this year there was a lot of layoffs in IT as AI had a devastating impact. That hype seems to have died down and now there appars to be more openings, at least in the IT field than there were 6 months ago, which in my opinion is a good thing, I just hope their not jobs to bring back people to further mature their AI replacements and a second round of layoffs is coming.

I saw a job posting recently for a job I have never officially done, but have participated in during the divestiture. I have no doubts that I could do it and do it well. The part that really attracted me to the position though is that on the website for the company, they have a large section about Job Stability. I cannot even begin to express the positive impact feeling job security would have on my personal stress!

I have my interview today at noon, so I'm working from home. This is easily the most nervous I have been for a job interview in over 10 years. Since I started my IT career all the interviews I have had have been for the same company to fill internal positions. They were still somewhat stressful, but ultimately they were the same company and not because I feel like I "need" the job.

I studied for the interview though, dressed in a button up shirt and tie, I didn't really feel like a suit was warranted since it was a remote interview via Teams, and tested my equipment. I know that when I interview people I'm less concerned with their ability to already know everything and every tool and I'm more worried about their atitude and friendliness. I can teach someone who wants to learn anything, but I can't make them give a damn!
